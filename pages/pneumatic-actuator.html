Pneumatic Actuator: Open and Close
Contact: Casey Glynn Lead Engineer

The pneumatic actuator is a double acting quarter turn device. Air fed to one port drives the disc out of the bore, air fed to the other port brings it back, and a flow control valve on each line sets how fast it gets there. The animation strokes the valve both ways and lights the line that is pressurized.

AIR TO OPEN, AIR TO CLOSE

The lit line is the one being fed. The cutaway in the middle is the disc in the bore, and the indicator on top of the actuator reads the same position.

WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT
  1. Know your ports before you pull tubing. The tubing connection drawing tells you which port is the closed port. Guessing swaps the action.
  2. Air off first. Shut the supply before any line comes off the actuator, then move the closed port line to the supply in on the air switch.
  3. Both ports on the switch. Move the open port line as well, feed the switch to both ports, then open the air and work the switch back and forth to check the stroke.
  4. Set the speed, not the travel. Loosen the lock nut, close each flow control valve clockwise, then open it 1.5 turns counterclockwise and trim until the operation is smooth.
  5. The stops are travel limits. Open stop and closed stop set where the disc lands, not how fast it moves. Leave them alone when you are chasing speed.
