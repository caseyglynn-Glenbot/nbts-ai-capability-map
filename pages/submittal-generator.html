HOW TO USE IT

Five fields, one upload. The generator handles the rest.

  1. Pick your product line below and the submittal form opens in a new tab.
  2. Enter your email. This is where the finished package is sent.
  3. Upload the quote PDF. Multi-pool projects and scanned, image-only quotes both work.
  4. Add the job number and engineer initials. Optionally add a project name to override the one read from the quote. These go on the submittal cover.
  5. Submit. In about 3-5 minutes the annotated package is emailed to you and filed to NBTS Jira.

HOW IT WORKS

The process end to end, in plain terms.

StepStageWhat happens
1Quote comes inA sales engineer submits the quote through the form and names the project.Needs: the quote PDF. Nothing else to fill out.
2The order is readEvery line item is pulled off the quote: part number, description, size and quantity, including multi system projects.Produces: a clean list of what was actually sold.
3Documents are matchedEach part is looked up against the product library and paired with its correct cut sheet, data sheet or drawing.Produces: the exact set of pages this project needs, in order.
4Selections are markedEvery sheet is annotated to show what the customer bought, so the engineer reviewing it never has to guess which row applies.Produces: red boxes on the ordered rows, yellow callouts on quantities and options.
5Package is assembledCover sheet, table of contents, tab dividers and all annotated pages merge into one submittal ready to send.Produces: a single finished PDF, typically 25 to 40 pages.
6A person approves itThe package is reviewed and released by our team. The generator prepares the work, it does not send anything to a customer on its own.Control point: nothing reaches the customer without sign off.

WHY IT MATTERS

What this changes for the business.

  • Faster response. Submittals that took an engineer half a day are ready in minutes.
  • Consistent output. The same rules apply to every quote, so packages do not vary by who built them.
  • Fewer misses. Items get flagged when no document exists for them, instead of quietly dropping off the package.
  • Engineering time back. Support engineers spend their day on customer problems, not assembling PDFs.
Human release required. Generated packages are reviewed before they go to a customer. Missing documents are reported on the run rather than silently skipped.
